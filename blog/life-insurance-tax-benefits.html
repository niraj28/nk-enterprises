Tax benefits on life insurance: 80C and 10(10D) in simple words

Every year around January, people start asking me: "Which policy will save my tax?" Tax savings are a useful bonus, but they should never be the only reason to buy insurance. Still, it is good to understand how the rules work so you don't get any surprises. Here is a plain-language overview.

A quick note on the law

For decades these benefits have been known by their section numbers in the Income Tax Act, 1961: Section 80C for the deduction on premiums and Section 10(10D) for tax-free payouts. A new Income-tax Act, 2025 has replaced the old Act from 1 April 2026, and section numbers have changed. Most people, and this article, still use the familiar names. Tax rules also change in Budgets, so please confirm the current position with your CA or tax adviser before filing.

Benefit 1: deduction on premiums (80C)

  • Premiums you pay for life insurance on yourself, your spouse or your children can be deducted from your taxable income.
  • The overall limit is ₹1.5 lakh a year, and this limit is shared with other items such as PPF, EPF, ELSS, home loan principal and children's tuition fees.
  • For policies issued on or after 1 April 2012, the deduction is allowed only up to 10% of the sum assured each year. If your premium is higher than that, the extra part does not count. (Older policies had a 20% limit, and there are relaxations for persons with disability or certain illnesses.)
  • If you stop paying and the policy ends within the first two years, the deductions already claimed can be added back to your income.

Important: old regime vs new regime

The 80C deduction is available only if you choose the old tax regime. The new tax regime, which is now the default, has lower tax rates but does not allow this deduction. Many salaried people are better off in the new regime even without 80C. Work out both before deciding, and remember: if you are in the new regime, the premium deduction does not help you, but the payout exemption below can still apply.

Benefit 2: tax-free payouts (10(10D))

Death claim: money paid to the family on the death of the life assured is fully exempt from tax.

Maturity and survival benefits are exempt only if certain conditions are met:

  • For policies issued on or after 1 April 2012, the premium in any year should not be more than 10% of the sum assured (20% for policies issued between April 2003 and March 2012).
  • For traditional policies issued on or after 1 April 2023, if the total premium of all such policies you hold is more than ₹5 lakh a year, the maturity amount of those policies is taxable on the gain. Unit-linked plans have a separate ₹2.5 lakh limit for policies issued from February 2021.

When the payout is not exempt, the insurer may deduct TDS on the income part. Make sure your PAN is updated in your policy records.

Pension plans

Premiums paid for certain pension plans fall under Section 80CCC, which is part of the same ₹1.5 lakh overall limit. The tax treatment of pension income is different from regular life insurance, so check the specific plan's terms.

A simple example

Suppose Ramesh, in the old regime, buys a policy with a sum assured of ₹5 lakh and a yearly premium of ₹40,000. Ten percent of the sum assured is ₹50,000, so his full premium is within the limit. He can claim ₹40,000 under 80C (if his other investments have not already used up the ₹1.5 lakh), and if his total yearly premiums stay below ₹5 lakh, his maturity amount should be tax-free too. If his premium had been ₹60,000, only ₹50,000 would count for the deduction.

Buy for protection first

The biggest "tax benefit" of life insurance is that it protects your family. Choose the right cover and plan for your needs first; let tax savings be a bonus, not the reason.
