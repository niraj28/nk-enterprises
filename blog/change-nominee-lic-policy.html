How to change the nominee in your LIC policy

The nominee is the person who receives the policy money if something happens to you. Many people fill this in once when buying the policy and never look at it again. But life changes: you get married, have children, or a parent you had named passes away. An outdated nominee can cause real difficulty for your family at the worst possible time. The good news is that changing it is simple.

When should you review your nominee?

  • After marriage, when many people want to name their spouse
  • After the birth of a child
  • If the current nominee has passed away
  • After a divorce or separation
  • If you want to split the money between more than one person

A good habit is to check nominee details on all family policies once every year or two.

Who can be a nominee?

Usually a close family member: spouse, children, parents or siblings. You can name more than one nominee and state what share each one should get. If you don't mention shares, ask LIC how the money would be divided.

Under the Insurance Act, if you nominate your parents, spouse or children (or any of them), they are treated as beneficial nominees, meaning the money belongs to them and not just held for other legal heirs. For other nominees, legal heirs may still have a claim. If your family situation is complicated, it is worth getting legal advice.

If the nominee is a minor

A child cannot receive the money directly. When you name a minor as nominee, you must also name an appointee: an adult, often the other parent or a trusted relative, who will receive the money on the child's behalf until the child turns 18. The appointee must sign their consent on the form. This is a step people often forget.

How to change the nominee: step by step

  1. Get the nomination form from your servicing branch or your agent. LIC may also allow nomination changes online for some policies through the customer portal; check there first.
  2. Fill in the new nominee's details: full name exactly as on their ID, relationship to you, age or date of birth, and share if there are several nominees.
  3. Add an appointee if any nominee is a minor.
  4. Sign the form. The policyholder must sign; a witness may be needed.
  5. Submit it at the servicing branch along with your ID proof. LIC may ask for the original policy bond to make an endorsement on it.
  6. Collect the acknowledgement. LIC registers the change and confirms it. A small fee may apply.

The new nomination replaces the old one completely, so list everyone you want to include, not only the new person.

Special situations

Policies under the Married Women's Property Act (MWP Act): if your policy was taken under this Act for your wife and children, it works like a trust. The beneficiaries cannot be changed by a simple nomination form. Ask LIC about the rules.

Assigned policies: if you have assigned the policy to a bank or someone else, the assignment affects the nomination. Check with LIC before making changes.

Only the policyholder can change the nominee. If the person who is insured is different from the proposer (for example, a parent who took a policy on a child), ask LIC who is allowed to sign.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Spelling the nominee's name differently from their Aadhaar or bank account
  • Forgetting to appoint an appointee for a minor
  • Not telling the nominee that the policy exists or where the bond is kept
  • Assuming that a will automatically changes the nominee; please discuss with a lawyer how your will and nomination work together
