How to read your LIC policy bond

When a new policy arrives, most people check their name, put the bond in a safe place, and never read it. But this document is your contract with LIC. It tells you exactly what you have bought, what you must pay and what your family will receive. Spending twenty minutes with it can save a lot of confusion later. Here is how to read it.

1. The policy schedule (the first pages)

The schedule is the summary of your policy. Check every line carefully:

  • Policy number: you will need this for everything, so note it down separately too.
  • Name, date of birth and address of the life assured and the proposer. Spelling mistakes here can cause trouble at claim time.
  • Age and whether age is "admitted": if age proof was not accepted at the start, you may need to give it later.
  • Plan name and number: tells you which LIC plan you have.
  • Date of commencement and date of maturity.
  • Policy term and premium paying term: these can be different. Some plans let you stop paying premiums before the policy ends.

2. Sum assured and benefits

Look for the basic sum assured and the description of benefits:

  • Death benefit: what your nominee gets if you die during the term.
  • Maturity benefit: what you get if you survive the term, if the plan has one.
  • Survival benefits: in money-back plans, instalments paid at set intervals.
  • Whether the policy is with profits (participates in bonuses) or without profits.

Bonuses are not printed as a fixed number in advance; they are declared by LIC from time to time.

3. Premium details

  • Instalment premium and mode (yearly, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly)
  • Due dates, which are linked to your date of commencement
  • Date of last premium: after this, no more premiums are due

Taxes such as GST are charged on top of the premium in many plans. Your receipts show the breakup.

4. Nominee and assignment

The schedule shows who the nominee is, their relationship to you, and an appointee if the nominee is a minor. If the policy was assigned (for example, to a bank), that is recorded too. If the nominee details are wrong or outdated, get them corrected now.

5. Riders

If you added riders, such as accident benefit, term rider, critical illness or premium waiver, they are listed with their own sum assured and premium. Read what each rider covers and, just as important, what it excludes.

6. Conditions and privileges

This is the part most people skip, but it answers most questions you will have later:

  • Grace period for late premiums
  • Revival rules if the policy lapses
  • Paid-up value and surrender value rules
  • Policy loan eligibility
  • Exclusions, for example the suicide clause in the early period

7. Free-look period

After you receive a new policy, you get a free-look period to review it. If you are not happy with the terms, you can return it within this period and get your premium back after small deductions allowed by the rules, such as stamp duty and charges for the days you were covered. Under current IRDAI rules this period is 30 days for most new policies; the exact period is stated in your bond. Use this time to read everything.

8. Grievance and contact details

The bond lists your servicing branch address, how to raise a complaint, and the Insurance Ombudsman details if a complaint is not resolved. Keep this information handy.

Simple habits that help

  • Keep a photocopy or phone photo of the schedule pages, separate from the original.
  • Tell your nominee where the original bond is kept.
  • Make a one-page list of all family policies: number, sum assured, due date, maturity date and nominee.
