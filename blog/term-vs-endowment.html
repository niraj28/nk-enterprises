Term insurance vs endowment plan: which one suits you?

The question I hear most often is: "Should I take a term plan or an endowment plan?" Both are life insurance, but they do very different jobs. Here is a simple way to think about it.

Term insurance: maximum protection, lowest cost

A term plan pays a large amount to your family if you pass away during the policy term. If you survive the term, there is usually no maturity payout. Because it is pure protection, the premium is low and the cover can be very high.

Good for: young earners, people with home or car loans, and anyone whose family depends on their income.

Endowment plan: protection plus savings

An endowment plan gives life cover and pays you a lump sum at maturity, often with bonuses added. Part of your premium goes into savings, so the premium is higher and the life cover is smaller for the same money.

Good for: people who want disciplined, low-risk savings for a goal such as a child's education, marriage or retirement.

How much cover do you need?

A common rule of thumb is life cover of at least 10 times your yearly income, plus any loans you have. For example, if you earn ₹6 lakh a year and have a ₹10 lakh home loan, aim for around ₹70 lakh of cover. A term plan is usually the most affordable way to reach that amount.

Can you have both?

Yes, and many families do. A practical approach is:

  1. Take a term plan first, for the big protection your family needs.
  2. Add an endowment or child plan for a specific goal, within a premium you can comfortably pay every year.
