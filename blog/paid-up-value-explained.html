What is paid-up value in an LIC policy?

Sometimes a family cannot continue paying premiums: a job loss, a business slowdown, a medical emergency. A common worry is, "Will I lose everything I have paid?" With many traditional LIC policies, the answer is no. Your policy may become a paid-up policy. Here is what that means.

Paid-up value in one line

If you stop paying premiums after the policy has run for a minimum period, the policy does not end. It continues until maturity with a reduced sum assured, in proportion to the premiums you have actually paid. That reduced amount is called the paid-up value or paid-up sum assured.

When does a policy acquire paid-up value?

A policy needs to have run long enough first. For many traditional plans this means premiums have been paid for at least two or three full years, but the exact rule depends on your plan. If you stop before that, the policy simply lapses and, unless revived, gives no benefit.

Term plans are pure protection and generally have no paid-up value. If you stop paying, the cover ends after the grace period.

How it is usually calculated

For a typical endowment-type policy, the basic idea is:

Paid-up sum assured = Basic sum assured × (Number of premiums paid ÷ Total number of premiums payable)

Bonuses that were already added to the policy while it was in force usually stay attached. Future bonuses generally stop once the policy becomes paid-up.

A worked example

Suppose Sunita has an endowment policy with:

  • Basic sum assured: ₹5,00,000
  • Term: 20 years, yearly premium
  • Premiums paid: 8 years, then she stops

Paid-up sum assured = ₹5,00,000 × 8 ÷ 20 = ₹2,00,000.

If, say, bonuses of ₹1,20,000 had already been added in those 8 years, Sunita (or her family) would receive about ₹2,00,000 plus that ₹1,20,000 of vested bonus, either at maturity or on her death before maturity, depending on the plan's terms. These figures are only for illustration; your own policy's numbers will differ.

Notice the difference: had she kept paying, the full ₹5 lakh plus 20 years of bonuses would have been payable. Stopping is not a total loss, but it is a big reduction.

Plans with special rules

Money-back plans deduct survival benefits already paid when working out the paid-up value. Some plans have their own formula, and newer plans may differ. The "Conditions and privileges" section of your policy bond explains how it works for your plan. If it is unclear, ask LIC for a paid-up value quotation.

What can you do with a paid-up policy?

  • Leave it as it is and receive the paid-up value at maturity.
  • Revive it: within the revival period allowed by your plan, you can pay the overdue premiums with interest and restore the full cover and bonuses. This is often the best option if your finances recover.
  • Take a loan: many paid-up policies still allow a loan against their surrender value, though usually a smaller percentage than in-force policies.
  • Surrender it for cash, if you really need the money. Compare carefully first; surrender usually gives less than waiting.

How to find your policy's paid-up value

You don't need to calculate it yourself. Log in to the LIC customer portal, ask at your servicing branch, or ask your agent for a paid-up value quotation. Keep your policy number handy and check the figure against your bond.

Paid-up vs lapsed: why it matters

A lapsed policy gives no cover. A paid-up policy gives reduced cover and a guaranteed amount at maturity. If you are struggling to pay, talk to your agent before simply stopping, so you know exactly which situation you will be in.
