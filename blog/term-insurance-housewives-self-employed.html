Term insurance for housewives and self-employed people

Most talk about term insurance is aimed at salaried people with a monthly payslip. But two large groups are often left out: homemakers, who run the house without a salary, and self-employed people such as shopkeepers, farmers, traders, doctors and small business owners. Both need protection, and both can face a few extra steps when buying it.

Part 1: Homemakers

Why a homemaker needs life cover

A homemaker may not earn a salary, but think about the work done every day: cooking, childcare, teaching children, caring for elderly parents, managing the household budget. If that person were no longer there, the family might need to pay for help with many of these tasks, and the working spouse may have to cut back on work. That is a real financial loss.

Term insurance on a homemaker gives the family money to manage that change without stress.

How insurers usually look at it

Because there is no income to measure, insurers generally decide a homemaker's cover by looking at the spouse's income and the spouse's own cover. Often the spouse needs to have a policy too, and the homemaker's cover may be limited to a part of the spouse's cover. Some insurers also look at the family's total income or the homemaker's own education. These rules differ between insurers and plans, so ask before you apply.

Practical tips

  • Apply together with the spouse's policy, which often makes the process smoother.
  • Keep the spouse's income proof (ITR or salary slips) ready.
  • Be honest about health in the proposal form, including pregnancy, thyroid, diabetes or any past surgery. Hiding information can lead to a claim being rejected later.

Part 2: Self-employed people

Why it matters even more

When a business owner passes away, the income often stops completely. There is no employer group cover and no provident fund. There may also be business loans or supplier dues. A term plan makes sure the family can clear debts and continue life without selling the shop or land in a hurry.

Proving income

Insurers set the maximum cover based on your income, so they will ask for proof. The most common documents are:

  • Income tax returns (ITR) for the last two or three years, with computation of income
  • Profit and loss statement and balance sheet, if you maintain them
  • Bank statements showing business receipts
  • GST returns, if registered

Filing your ITR regularly, even if your income is below the taxable limit, makes buying insurance (and loans) much easier. If your income is mostly agricultural, ask the insurer what proof they accept.

How much cover is sensible?

For self-employed people, a common starting point is around 10 times your yearly income plus all loans. If your income goes up and down, use an average of the last few years. For homemakers, the cover is usually lower and depends on the insurer's rules; think about what it would cost to run the house and care for the children for several years.

Things to keep in mind for both

  • Choose a policy term that lasts until children are independent and loans are paid off, often until 60 or 65.
  • Pay premiums on time; set up auto-debit if possible.
  • Name the right nominee and tell them about the policy.
  • Term plans are pure protection. If you also want savings, consider that separately.

Honesty in the proposal form

Whoever you are buying for, fill the proposal form truthfully: income, occupation, existing policies, smoking or tobacco use, and medical history. It may raise the premium a little, but it protects the claim. If the form is filled by someone else on your behalf, read it fully before you sign.
