LIC maturity claim: a step-by-step guide

The day your policy matures is a happy one. Years of disciplined saving finally come back to you as a lump sum. To make sure the money reaches your bank account on time, it helps to know the steps in advance. Here is how the maturity claim usually works.

Step 1: Know your maturity date

The maturity date is printed in the schedule of your policy bond. You can also see it on the LIC customer portal or app. Note it down somewhere you will remember, and make sure all premiums are paid until the end of the premium paying term. If a premium is pending, the maturity amount may be reduced.

Step 2: Watch for the intimation letter

A few months before maturity, LIC generally sends an intimation letter (and often an SMS or email) to the address and contact details in its records. This letter lists the documents to submit. If you have moved house or changed your mobile number, update your details with LIC well in advance, or the letter may not reach you.

Not received anything as the date comes close? Don't wait. Contact your servicing branch or agent.

Step 3: Gather the documents

The exact list is in the intimation letter, but commonly needed items are:

  1. Discharge form (sometimes called the discharge voucher), signed by the policyholder. If the policy was assigned, the assignee signs.
  2. Original policy bond. If it is lost, inform the branch; a separate process applies.
  3. Identity and address proof (KYC) such as Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID or passport.
  4. Bank details: an NEFT mandate form with a cancelled cheque or the passbook's first page showing your name, account number and IFSC.
  5. Age proof, if your age was not admitted when the policy was issued.

Check that the name on your bank account matches the name on the policy. A mismatch is one of the most common reasons for a delay.

Step 4: Submit before the maturity date

Submit the papers at your servicing branch, or through your agent, ideally a few weeks before the maturity date. Keep photocopies and ask for an acknowledgement. When everything is in order, LIC aims to credit the maturity amount directly to your bank account by NEFT on or around the maturity date.

Step 5: Check the amount you receive

For a traditional with-profit plan, the maturity amount is generally the basic sum assured plus the bonuses added over the years, and a final additional bonus if the plan declares one. A plan with money-back instalments will deduct the survival benefits already paid, as the policy document explains. If the amount looks different from what you expected, ask the branch for a calculation sheet. You have every right to understand it.

What about tax?

Maturity money from life insurance can be tax-free under Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act, but only if certain conditions are met, such as the yearly premium staying within a set percentage of the sum assured, and limits on total premium for policies issued from April 2023 onwards. If the amount is not exempt, LIC may deduct TDS on the income part and report it against your PAN. Make sure your PAN is linked to the policy. We have a separate guide on tax benefits; for your exact situation, please speak to a tax adviser.

Should you reinvest the money?

Before the money arrives, think about what it is for. If it is for a child's education or a wedding, keep it safe and easy to access. If you don't need it right away, consider options that match your age and comfort with risk. Please don't rush into any scheme promising very high returns; maturity time is when fraud calls often increase.
