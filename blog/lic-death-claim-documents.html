Documents needed for an LIC death claim: a simple checklist

Losing a family member is hard enough. The paperwork that follows should not add to the burden. This guide lists the documents usually needed for an LIC death claim, so the family can gather them calmly and in one go. You do not have to do this alone; your agent's job is to help you through it.

First step: inform LIC

Inform the branch that issued the policy (the servicing branch) as soon as you reasonably can. Your agent can do this for you. Share the policy number, the name of the policyholder, the date and cause of death, and the claimant's name. The branch will then tell you exactly which forms apply to your case.

If you are unsure which branch serviced the policy, it is printed on the policy bond and on premium receipts.

Documents usually needed in every death claim

  1. Claim form filled by the claimant (the claimant's statement), given by the branch.
  2. Death certificate issued by the local municipal body or the Registrar of Births and Deaths. Get several attested copies; banks and other offices will also ask for it.
  3. Original policy bond. If it is lost, tell the branch; there is a separate process with an indemnity.
  4. Claimant's identity and address proof (KYC), such as Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID or passport.
  5. Bank details for NEFT: a bank mandate form plus a cancelled cheque or the first page of the passbook in the claimant's name.
  6. Proof of age of the deceased, if age was not admitted when the policy was issued.

Extra documents if the policy was recent

If the death happened within the first few years of the policy (or of its revival), LIC usually asks for more information to confirm the details given at the time of the proposal. Commonly requested papers include:

  • A certificate from the doctor who attended the deceased during the last illness
  • Hospital treatment records, discharge summary, or a certificate from the hospital
  • A certificate of identity and cremation or burial from a known, responsible person
  • A certificate from the employer, if the deceased was employed

The branch will give you the exact forms. Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, after three years from the date of the policy (or its revival), an insurer cannot question the policy on any ground, so claims on older policies are usually simpler.

Extra documents for accidental or unnatural death

For a road accident, drowning, or any other unnatural death, LIC typically also needs:

  • A copy of the FIR (First Information Report)
  • The post-mortem report
  • The police inquest report or panchnama, and the final police report if available

If the policy had an accident benefit rider, these papers are also needed to claim that extra amount.

When there is no nominee

If there is a valid nominee, the claim is paid to the nominee. If no nominee was registered, or the nominee has died, the legal heirs need to prove their right to the money. This can involve a succession certificate or other legal documents, which takes time. This is one big reason to keep nominations up to date.

Practical tips for the family

  • Make a file and keep photocopies of every paper you submit.
  • Take an acknowledgement from the branch when you hand over documents.
  • Make sure the names on the death certificate, policy and ID proofs match. Small spelling differences can cause delays; the branch may ask for an affidavit if they differ.
  • Do not pay anyone to "speed up" a claim. Claim settlement is free.

IRDAI rules set time limits within which insurers must settle claims once all documents are received. If you feel a claim is delayed, ask the branch in writing for the status.
