Loan against your LIC policy, explained simply

Sometimes money is needed urgently: a hospital bill, school fees, a repair at home. Before you break your policy or take an expensive personal loan, check whether you can borrow against your LIC policy. It is often one of the simpler and cheaper ways to raise money, as long as you understand how it works.

What is a policy loan?

A policy loan is money LIC lends you using your own policy as security. You don't need a guarantor, and the policy keeps running. Your life cover continues as long as you keep paying premiums. Any loan that is still outstanding, plus interest, is simply deducted from the amount paid at maturity or on a claim.

Which policies are eligible?

  • Loans are available on many traditional savings plans, such as endowment and whole life plans, once they have acquired a surrender value.
  • A policy usually acquires surrender value after premiums have been paid for a minimum number of years, often two or three full years depending on the plan.
  • Pure term plans have no surrender value, so no loan is available on them.
  • Some plans, such as certain money-back plans, have their own loan rules.

The "Conditions and privileges" section of your policy bond says whether a loan is allowed. If it is not clear, ask LIC or your agent.

How much can you borrow?

The loan amount is a percentage of the surrender value, not of the sum assured. For many LIC policies this is up to around 90% of the surrender value if the policy is in force, and a lower percentage if it is paid-up. Since surrender value is low in the early years, the loan available is also small at first and grows as the policy gets older. The exact eligible amount for your policy is shown on the customer portal or can be given by the branch.

How does interest work?

  • LIC charges interest at a rate it announces from time to time. Check the current rate before you apply.
  • Interest is usually payable every six months.
  • You can repay the loan in full or in parts whenever you like, or just keep paying interest and let the loan be adjusted at maturity.

The risk to understand

If you don't pay the interest, it gets added to the loan. Over time, the total of loan plus unpaid interest can grow larger than the surrender value. When that happens, LIC can foreclose the policy: it adjusts the surrender value against the loan and the policy ends. You would lose your life cover and future bonuses.

So the golden rule is simple: keep paying the interest and the premiums, and repay the loan when you can.

How to apply

  1. Check eligibility and the loan amount on the LIC customer portal, or ask your agent.
  2. For eligible policies, you can apply online through the portal. Otherwise, submit the loan form at your servicing branch.
  3. Keep ready your original policy bond, KYC documents and bank details (cancelled cheque or passbook copy).
  4. The policy is assigned to LIC as security for the loan. The money is credited to your bank account once processed.

After you repay the loan fully, the policy is reassigned to you.

Policy loan or personal loan?

A policy loan generally needs no credit score check, has simple paperwork and allows flexible repayment. Personal loans often carry higher interest and fixed EMIs. On the other hand, a policy loan reduces the money your family receives if it is not repaid. Borrow only what you need and plan how you will pay it back.
