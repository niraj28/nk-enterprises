How to choose a child education plan

Every parent dreams of giving their child the best education. But college fees, coaching, hostel costs and professional courses keep getting more expensive every year. A child education plan is one way to build that money steadily. Here is how to think about it, so you choose a plan that actually fits your family.

Step 1: Estimate how much you will need

Start with the goal, not the product. Ask yourself:

  • What course might your child study? Engineering, medicine, a degree, a professional course?
  • When will the money be needed? If your child is 5 today, college fees start in about 12–13 years.
  • What does that course cost today? Check a few colleges' fee pages.

Education costs tend to rise faster than general prices. As a rough way to plan, assume today's cost could be well over double by the time your child is 18. It is only an estimate, but it stops you from planning with today's prices.

Step 2: Protect the plan, not just the savings

The real job of a child plan is this: even if the parent is not there, the child's education money must still arrive. That is why the most important feature to look for is a premium waiver benefit.

With a premium waiver, if the parent who pays the premiums dies during the term, the future premiums are waived and the policy continues. The child still receives the planned payouts on time. In some plans this is built in; in others it is an optional rider you add for a small extra cost. Read the benefit carefully to see whose life the waiver covers.

Alongside this, the earning parent should also have enough term insurance. A child plan alone usually cannot replace a parent's income.

Step 3: Match payouts to fee years

Child plans pay money in different ways. Some pay a lump sum at the end; others pay instalments at specific ages, for example when the child turns 18, 20, 22 and so on. Choose a structure that matches when fees are actually due. A single payout at 25 does not help if engineering fees start at 18.

Step 4: Choose a premium you can keep paying

These are long plans, often 15 years or more. Stopping midway usually means a loss. Pick a premium that fits comfortably even in a difficult year. If your income is irregular, a yearly or half-yearly premium may be easier than monthly. It is better to start a modest plan you can complete than a big one you have to abandon.

Step 5: Understand returns honestly

Traditional child plans offer safety and guarantees on some benefits, but their returns are generally modest. Bonuses on with-profit plans are not guaranteed in advance and depend on LIC's declarations. Market-linked plans can grow more but can also fall. Many families combine a safe insurance plan with other savings such as Sukanya Samriddhi (for a girl child), PPF or mutual funds, depending on their comfort with risk.

Questions to ask before you buy

  1. Who is the life assured: the parent or the child? Whose death triggers which benefit?
  2. Is there a premium waiver, and is it built in or a rider?
  3. Exactly when and how much will the plan pay out?
  4. What is guaranteed, and what depends on bonuses?
  5. What happens if I stop paying after a few years?
  6. What is the total premium I will pay over the full term?

Ask for a benefit illustration and read the policy document. A good agent will answer every one of these questions clearly.

Start early

The earlier you start, the smaller the premium needed to reach the same goal, and the more time the money has to grow. Even starting when your child is very young with a small amount makes a big difference later.
