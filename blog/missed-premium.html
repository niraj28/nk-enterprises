Missed your LIC premium? Grace period and revival explained

Life gets busy, and sometimes a premium date slips by. The good news: missing one due date does not end your policy straight away. Here is what happens and what you should do.

1. The grace period

After the due date, you get extra time to pay without losing your cover. This is called the grace period. For most LIC policies it is:

  • 15 days if you pay monthly
  • 30 days if you pay quarterly, half-yearly or yearly

Your life cover continues during the grace period. Pay within this time and nothing changes.

2. After the grace period: the policy lapses

If the premium is still unpaid when the grace period ends, the policy lapses. That means the full life cover stops, which is the biggest risk for your family.

If you have already paid premiums for the minimum number of years mentioned in your policy, it may become a paid-up policy with a reduced sum assured instead of lapsing completely. The exact rules depend on your plan.

3. Reviving a lapsed policy

You can usually bring a lapsed policy back, called revival, within a set period from the first unpaid premium (often up to five years for many plans). To revive, you generally need to:

  1. Pay all the overdue premiums.
  2. Pay interest on the late amount.
  3. Submit a health declaration, and in some cases a medical report, depending on how long the policy has been lapsed and the sum assured.

The sooner you revive, the simpler and cheaper it is. LIC also runs special revival campaigns from time to time with concessions on late fees.

How to avoid missing a premium

  • Set up auto-debit (NACH/ECS) from your bank account.
  • Save the due dates in your phone calendar with a reminder a week before.
  • Ask your agent for reminders. I send all my clients a WhatsApp message before every due date.
